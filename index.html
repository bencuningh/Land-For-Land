Phoenix Races

Somewhere in all those start lines, I fell in love with the events themselves, not just the racing. So I built my own: a backyard ultra, from a borrowed field to a real, growing race. A plan, not a pitch

Where I’d start at Land for Land

Five pillars, the same ones from your own listing. Not everything I’d do, just where I’d put the first ninety days.

01

Guest experience

  1. 1.Walk every guest journey myself, end to end, before asking anyone else to fix it.
  2. 2.Turn recurring small complaints into fixed standards within days, the discipline a race needs on the morning of the start.
  3. 3.Build a feedback loop that reaches me directly, not filtered through three layers of reporting.
02

Operations

  1. 1.Write the playbooks that don’t exist yet: the ones Redesk taught me you invent from scratch when there’s no template.
  2. 2.Put real unit economics behind every decision, sized right for a business this stage, not built for a bigger one.
  3. 3.Run a tight weekly rhythm (clear owners, clear numbers), the way Joko trained into me.
03

Product / commerce

  1. 1.Treat the offer like a race course: designed on purpose, tested by me first.
  2. 2.Find the highest-leverage commerce moves early (pricing, packaging, upsell) and ship the first version fast.
  3. 3.Iterate in public with guests, the way I grew Phoenix Races from a borrowed field into a real race.
04

Brand

  1. 1.Protect “exclusive because it’s good,” never “exclusive for its own sake.” That’s the standard I built Phoenix Races on.
  2. 2.Make every operational decision legible in the brand: guests should feel the standards, not just read about them.
  3. 3.Say no to anything that grows the brand faster than it grows trust.
05

Business

  1. 1.Get to a clear, honest read on unit economics fast, no vanity metrics.
  2. 2.Bring the financial and operational rigor a real cofounder owes the business, the kind Nicolas modeled for me.
  3. 3.Build Land for Land into something desirable and profitable, not a trade-off between the two.
